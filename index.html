Problem: both work inefficiently.

Forward flows are contractive.

We measure how much a small change in the input noise changes the action a standard flow policy (Vanilla CFM) outputs. The change in the action is far smaller than the change in the noise, which leaves RL that acts on the noise little to explore.

The reverse process is expansive.

  • Each dot is one demonstration's action, mapped back through the flow to the noise that produces it.
  • For Vanilla CFM, the noises of Pick Mug and Pick Bowl demonstrations are mixed together, although their actions are clearly separated.
  • A noise policy trained on these noises (DSBC) has to fit a target that switches between tasks over short distances in noise space.

Don't let any denoising step contract too much.

LoLip adds one term to the flow-matching loss: for a random unit direction u, it penalizes any denoising step that scales u by less than smin.

Shape the noise → action map during pretraining.

PROBLEM
Standard flow policies are highly contractive: moving the noise barely moves the action, which hurts both RL and supervised steering in latent space.
CAUSE
Flow matching fits the action distribution but never shapes the noise → action map.
FIX
LoLip lower-bounds the singular values of each denoising step's Jacobian, which lower-bounds the stretch of the whole flow.
RESULT
A less contractive forward map and more stable inversion give better supervised steering (DSBC) and latent-space RL (DSRL).

LoLip costs some base performance. DSBC wins it back.

LIBERO-90, 20 tasks, base policies trained with the task instruction. Success rate of the base policy alone: Vanilla CFM 86%, LoLip 70%. With a DSBC noise policy trained on the same demonstrations: 89% and 85%.
