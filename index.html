Problem: both work inefficiently.

Problem 1: Forward flows are highly contractive.

We pick 8 noises far apart in the latent space and run the standard flow policy (Vanilla CFM) from each. The 8 resulting actions are nearly identical, and so are the 8 robot motions. RL that acts on the noise therefore has little to explore.

Across all noise directions, the median singular value of ∂a/∂z is 0.0037: a change in the noise is typically scaled down by a factor of about 270 in the action.

Problem 2: The reverse process is expansive.

To steer the policy from demonstrations (DSBC), each demonstrated action is mapped back through the flow to the noise that produces it, and a noise policy is fit to those noises. For Vanilla CFM, the Pick Mug and Pick Bowl noises are mixed together although their actions are clearly separated.

Asked to pick the mug, the noise policy samples from a region that also holds bowl noises, and here it selects one: the robot reaches for the bowl.

Idea: don't let any denoising step contract too much.

LoLip adds one term to the flow-matching loss: for a random unit direction u, it penalizes any denoising step that scales u by less than smin.

Fixing Problem 1: LoLip makes the forward flow less contractive.

The same 8 noises, run through the LoLip policy, give 8 clearly different actions and robot motions.

On LIBERO-Goal, sampling 256 noises at a fixed robot state, LoLip's action standard deviation is 1.95–2.12 times that of Vanilla CFM on each of the 4 tasks.

Fixing Problem 2: LoLip separates tasks in noise space.

With LoLip, the Pick Mug noises form their own region. The noise policy fit to them samples a mug noise, and the robot reaches for the mug.

Shape the noise → action map during pretraining.

PROBLEM
Standard flow policies are highly contractive: moving the noise barely moves the action, which hurts both RL and supervised steering in latent space.
CAUSE
Flow matching fits the action distribution but never shapes the noise → action map.
FIX
LoLip lower-bounds the singular values of each denoising step's Jacobian, which lower-bounds the stretch of the whole flow.
RESULT
A less contractive forward map and more stable inversion give better supervised steering (DSBC) and latent-space RL (DSRL).

Limitation: LoLip lowers base performance; DSBC recovers most of it.

LIBERO-90, 20 tasks, base policies trained with the task instruction. Success rate of the base policy alone: Vanilla CFM 86%, LoLip 70%. With a DSBC noise policy trained on the same demonstrations: 89% and 85%.
